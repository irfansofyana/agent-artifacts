Estimated study load: 45–60 minutes/day for six weeks.

Your first goal is not daily profit.

Markets do not owe you a paycheck every day. A daily-profit target pushes beginners toward overtrading, oversized leverage, and revenge trades. Your real first goal is to execute a tested process with small, predefined losses. A legitimate trading day may end positive, negative, flat—or with no trade at all.

01 / Reset the goal

Measure decisions, not today’s money.

Profit is a noisy outcome. Process quality is trainable. For the first six weeks, score yourself on rule-following and data collection.

0

Guaranteed days

No strategy wins every day. Anyone promising guaranteed returns is selling a fantasy or a scam.

1

Setup to master

Do not collect indicators. Trade one pair, one session, one repeatable setup.

40+

Demo trades first

A small sample is not proof, but it is enough to expose whether you can follow your own rules.

03 / First setup

Set up a clean training environment.

You need a demo account, a journal, and one chart. Nothing else. Do not buy signals, robots, VIP groups, or a funded-account challenge.

Verify the company

Match brand, legal company name, and license in the live Bappebti registry. Avoid APK files and links sent through chat.

Create demo only

Do not deposit yet. Use your own email, strong password, and official app-store link from the broker website.

Choose EUR/USD

Start with one major pair. It is widely followed and usually more liquid than exotic pairs, but costs still vary by broker and time.

Use two chart frames

H1 for direction and M15 for the entry. Add only 20 EMA and 50 EMA as visual trend guides.

Fix one session

Trade a consistent 60–90 minute window during London or London/New York overlap. Session times shift with daylight saving; check your platform clock.

Open the journal

Record the plan before entry, screenshot before/after, risk in R, result in R, rule adherence, and emotional state.

04 / Market language

Know what can hurt you.

Learn these before placing even a demo order. Tap each term to expand it.

Currency pair

EUR/USD quotes euros in US dollars. Buying means you expect EUR to strengthen relative to USD; selling means the opposite.

Pip

A standard unit of price movement, commonly the fourth decimal place for many pairs. Pip value depends on pair, trade size, price, and account currency.

Lot / position size

The quantity you trade. The broker's minimum increment matters. Position size—not confidence—is how you control financial risk.

Leverage and margin

Leverage lets a small margin control a larger position. It magnifies losses as efficiently as gains. Margin is collateral, not the maximum you can lose.

Spread, commission, swap

Spread is the bid/ask gap; commission may be charged separately; swap is overnight financing. Your strategy must beat all trading costs.

Slippage

Your fill can differ from the requested price, especially around news or thin liquidity. A stop is risk control, not a guaranteed exact fill.

Market, limit, stop orders

Market orders prioritize execution; limit orders seek a better price; stop orders activate after a trigger. Learn entry and protective-stop behavior in demo.

R-multiple

R is your predefined risk. Lose the planned amount = −1R. Make 1.5 times that amount = +1.5R. This normalizes results across account sizes.

05 / Risk engine

Survival is the strategy underneath the strategy.

For learning, cap risk at 0.25%–0.50% per trade, stop after −1.5% in a day, and stop for the week at −3%. Limits are ceilings, not targets.

06 / One playbook

Trend pullback, not prediction.

This is a training hypothesis to test—not a magic strategy. Use it unchanged for at least 40 valid demo trades before judging it.

Long setup

  • Context: H1 20 EMA above 50 EMA; price structure shows higher highs and higher lows.
  • Location: On M15, price pulls back toward recent support or the EMA area without breaking the H1 structure.
  • Trigger: A bullish M15 candle closes back in the trend direction. Enter only after the candle closes.
  • Stop: Below the pullback swing low, at a level that proves the setup wrong.
  • Target: Next resistance with at least 1.5R potential. Skip if space is insufficient.

Short setup

  • Context: H1 20 EMA below 50 EMA; price structure shows lower highs and lower lows.
  • Location: On M15, price pulls back toward recent resistance or the EMA area without breaking H1 structure.
  • Trigger: A bearish M15 candle closes back in the trend direction. Enter only after the candle closes.
  • Stop: Above the pullback swing high, where the setup is invalidated.
  • Target: Next support with at least 1.5R potential. Skip if the chart is cramped.

Hard filters

These reduce bad decisions; they do not guarantee good outcomes.

No trade if: high-impact EUR or USD news is near; spread is abnormal; you missed the planned entry; target offers less than 1.5R; you have already lost two trades; you are tired, angry, rushing, or trying to recover money.
07 / Six-week course

Learn in the right order.

Do not compress this into a weekend. Familiarity is not competence.

WEEK 01

Mechanics

  • Complete broker demo onboarding
  • Learn pair, pip, lot, spread, margin
  • Practice market/limit/stop orders
  • Place 10 no-stakes practice orders
WEEK 02

Charts & risk

  • Mark trend and swing structure
  • Calculate R and risk budget
  • Place stop before target
  • Never move stop farther away
WEEK 03

Replay

  • Study 20 historical examples
  • Screenshot valid and invalid setups
  • Apply the same checklist
  • Ignore P&L; score rule clarity
WEEK 04

Demo execution

  • Trade one fixed session
  • Maximum two trades/day
  • Record every valid and skipped trade
  • Stop at daily loss limit
WEEK 05

Build evidence

  • Continue toward 40 valid trades
  • Calculate win rate and expectancy
  • Tag every rule violation
  • Do not change rules mid-sample
WEEK 06

Audit

  • Review screenshots in batches
  • Separate strategy loss from mistake
  • Check drawdown and costs
  • Pass—or repeat—the graduation gate

09 / Daily operating system

Trade less. Review more.

A routine prevents each candle from renegotiating your rules.

Before / 15 min

Prepare

  1. Check high-impact calendar events.
  2. Mark H1 trend, support, resistance.
  3. Write allowed setup and invalidation.
  4. Set today’s loss limit.
During / 60–90 min

Execute

  1. Wait for a closed-candle trigger.
  2. Calculate size from stop distance.
  3. Maximum two trades.
  4. No moving stop farther; no adding to loss.
After / 15 min

Debrief

  1. Save before/after screenshots.
  2. Record result in R.
  3. Score every rule yes/no.
  4. Write one lesson—no strategy edits today.
10 / Go-live gate

Real money is a privilege, not week seven.

If any gate fails, continue demo. There is no deadline and no shame in repeating the course.

All six must be true

Passing does not mean you will profit. It means you have a minimally defensible process.

  1. At least 40 valid, fully journaled demo trades.
  2. Positive expectancy after realistic spread/commission/slippage assumptions.
  3. No risk-limit violation for 10 consecutive trading days.
  4. Maximum drawdown is understood and emotionally tolerable.
  5. No martingale, averaging down, borrowed money, or money needed within 12 months.
  6. You will start at the broker’s smallest practical size and accept that no-trade days are normal.

Immediate stop signs

  • You need trading to pay this month’s bills.
  • You hide losses or deposits from family.
  • You increase size after a loss.
  • You cannot sleep because of an open position.
  • You follow anonymous signal groups.
  • You move stops to avoid being wrong.
  • You deposit through a personal bank account.
  • You are promised fixed or guaranteed returns.
